# Porsurf Organizer Workflow

This is a reconstructed timeline of how the 2026 Sri Lanka trip was organized. It turns the Telegram history into a reusable action workflow for future organizer teams.

## Overview

- Planning began: November 11, 2025
- Registration filled: December 1, 2025
- Main trip: May 4 to May 12, 2026
- Organizer team: six people
- Participant capacity: approximately 25 participants plus organizers
- Initial advertised price: EUR 1,150 to EUR 1,250

The process worked through short organizer meetings, a shared Drive, a working budget, named owners, participant announcement channels, and increasingly detailed task lists as departure approached.

## Phase 1: Define the trip

### November 11 to 12, around six months before departure

The team agreed on the first version of the trip:

- Destination: Arugam Bay, Sri Lanka
- Dates: May 4 to May 12, 2026
- Capacity and participant allocation
- Target price and what it should include
- Beginner-friendly positioning
- Core program: surf lessons, swimming, snorkeling, sitsit, and surprise activities

The first responsibilities were assigned immediately:

- Launch message and copy
- Registration setup
- Campaign image and video
- Surf camp sourcing and comparison

**Output:** A sellable trip concept with dates, capacity, price range, positioning, and initial owners.

## Phase 2: Build the launch

### November 13 to 30, five and a half months before departure

The team created the launch materials and registration process:

- Drafted the announcement around history, warmth, surfing, and adventure.
- Made it explicit that no previous surf experience was required.
- Collected and created visual assets.
- Set the queue and registration mechanics.
- Scheduled promotion to the student community.
- Reviewed a travel security notice and checked whether it reflected a current threat.
- Planned participant communications, payments, flights, surf bookings, and organizer roles before registration closed.
- Built a shared budget sheet and researched Sri Lanka.
- Assigned shifts for managing the physical registration queue.

**Output:** Launch campaign, registration process, initial risk review, shared workspace, and a preliminary budget.

## Phase 3: Convert demand into commitments

### December 1 to 17, five months before departure

Registration filled all 25 participant spots on December 1.

The team then moved quickly from interest to commitment:

- Secured a EUR 20,000 short-term bridge loan to book flights before all participant payments arrived.
- Created a formal announcement channel for important information.
- Asked every selected participant for final confirmation.
- Communicated that cancellation would no longer be possible after confirmation.
- Collected a first EUR 400 installment, with flexibility available when needed.
- Filled places created by cancellations.
- Booked flights and collected the required participant details.
- Flagged the need for Sri Lankan travel authorization.
- Continued maintaining the master budget.

**Output:** Confirmed participant group, flight funding, booked travel, payment process, and official communication channel.

## Phase 4: Lock the core supplier

### January 7 to February 18, three to four months before departure

The team negotiated directly with the surf and accommodation provider.

Topics confirmed included:

- Accommodation capacity and room types
- Breakfasts and selected dinners
- Five surf days
- Boards for beginners and more experienced surfers
- Daily surf spot selection and transport
- Yoga and excursion options
- Daily timings
- What transport was included
- Price and deposit structure

By January 30, the main local package was quoted at approximately USD 370 per person excluding airport transfer. A USD 1,170 advance payment was required to confirm it.

The organizers also:

- Finalized the remaining participant payment.
- Sent ETA application instructions with a February 15 deadline.
- Set April 14 as the PreSurf event date.
- Began planning merchandise and extra program.

**Output:** Confirmed surf camp package, deposit, activity framework, visa process, and final participant payment plan.

## Phase 5: Build identity and participant readiness

### February 19 to April 14, one and a half to three months before departure

The focus shifted from purchasing the trip to shaping the experience.

The team:

- Designed and ordered trip shirts.
- Collected sizes through a form and reconciled duplicate or missing answers.
- Monitored ETA approvals.
- Planned the PreSurf evening.
- Created a survival guide covering packing, safety, money, health, local culture, food, and surf basics.
- Produced playful surf training content, a quiz, and teaser media.
- Confirmed the PreSurf venue, attendance, snacks, drinks, sauna, presentation, and equipment.

The PreSurf evening on April 14 combined:

1. Introductions and participant expectations
2. Practical travel information and questions
3. Surf and destination orientation
4. Humor, quizzes, video, snacks, and sauna

**Output:** Prepared participants, established group identity, and resolved common questions before the final operational sprint.

## Phase 6: Final operations sprint

### April 15 to May 3, the final three weeks

The team reviewed every remaining dependency and assigned explicit deadlines.

### Participant and travel administration

- Finish and distribute the survival guide.
- Confirm the final participant list.
- Resolve passport and ETA edge cases.
- Send flight and accommodation details.
- Prepare room allocations.
- Complete the Ministry for Foreign Affairs travel notification.
- Confirm airport and local transfers.
- Prepare a participant waiver.
- Check online check-in limitations and airport procedures.

### On-trip operating model

- Create an organizer responsibility rotation.
- Assign a communications lead for each day of the trip.
- Finalize the itinerary while keeping surprise elements private.
- Prepare an organizer packing list and shared equipment.
- Reconfirm surf schedules, meals, yoga, transport, Wi-Fi, safety boxes, bars, and optional activities with the local provider.

### Experience design

Each organizer brought several memorable program ideas to an ideation lunch on April 27. The team then assigned owners for:

- Trip playlist, song, and opening ritual
- Sunrise tuk-tuk or off-road excursion
- Diving research
- Trip bingo and prizes
- A rotating comic award
- Local bar challenge
- Chant and group identity
- Teaser content, interviews, and aftermovie
- Tropical sitsit and other surprise programming

**Output:** Final rooming, travel documentation, organizer rotations, confirmed suppliers, packed shared equipment, and owned program modules.

## Phase 7: Departure day

### May 4, 2026

The airport briefing covered:

- Last-minute shopping window
- Signing participant waivers
- Explaining the organizer responsibility rotation
- Distributing trip shirts
- Checking that people, luggage, and shared equipment were accounted for

**Output:** The full group departed with the essential administration completed and a clear organizer structure.

## Phase 8: Run the trip adaptively

### May 5 to 12, 2026

The organizers used the itinerary as a framework rather than a rigid script.

- Surf groups and breakfast timing were coordinated around ability and capacity.
- The opening ritual, song, and dance established the shared identity.
- Social activities were adjusted based on weather and which venues were open.
- Local information was used to choose bars, surf spots, and activities.
- Organizers arrived early to inspect major events such as the sitsit.
- The daily communications lead kept participants informed.
- Photos and videos were gathered for group memories and the aftermovie.

**Output:** A structured but flexible trip that could react to real conditions without losing momentum.

## Phase 9: Close and carry momentum forward

### June to September 2026

After the trip, the team:

- Began editing a five to ten minute aftermovie.
- Discussed whether early May should remain the standard timing.
- Started destination and flight research for the next edition.
- Planned to begin the next marketing cycle in October.
- Reconfirmed organizer interest for the following year.

**Output:** Retained community energy, reusable media, and an early start for the next trip.

## Reusable organizer roles

Roles moved between people in 2026, but the work consistently fell into these lanes:

| Role | Main responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Trip lead | Timeline, meetings, task ownership, participant announcements, supplier coordination |
| Finance and administration | Budget, loan, payment collection, invoices, waivers, reconciliation |
| Travel and logistics | Flights, participant data, transfers, rooming, travel notifications, edge cases |
| Surf and safety | Provider comparison, surf program, equipment, safety guidance, local conditions |
| Participant experience | Pre-event, group identity, games, rituals, sitsit, surprises, daily energy |
| Creative and media | Launch assets, shirts, teasers, photography, interviews, aftermovie |

One person can own multiple lanes, but every lane needs a clearly named owner and deadline.

## Recommended operating cadence

### Six to four months before

- Meet every two to three weeks.
- Maintain one master budget and task list.
- Prioritize destination, demand validation, flights, funding, and the core supplier.

### Three to two months before

- Meet every two weeks.
- Track payments, visas, supplier details, merchandise, and participant readiness.

### Final six weeks

- Meet weekly.
- Give every task one owner and a calendar date.
- Maintain a written list of unresolved risks and participant questions.

### Final two weeks

- Use short check-ins as needed.
- Reconfirm every external dependency.
- Assign daily on-trip responsibility before departure.

### During the trip

- Hold a short organizer sync each day.
- Confirm the next day's surf, transport, meals, program, weather, and communications.
- Keep one person responsible for the participant update.

## Practical lessons from the transcript

- Book flights quickly once demand is confirmed. A bridge-financing plan may be necessary.
- Separate the official announcement channel from casual group conversation.
- State cancellation and payment terms before money is committed.
- Track participant data, payments, visas, and merchandise in one source of truth.
- Put access rights and document ownership in order early.
- Use local providers for condition-dependent decisions, but reconfirm all details in writing.
- Plan the emotional experience with the same seriousness as flights and accommodation.
- Preserve mystery around the program, but never around safety, money, or required participant actions.
- Expect cancellations, passport issues, missing form responses, schedule changes, and venue uncertainty.
- Assign a single owner to each task. Shared ownership without a lead tends to become delayed ownership.
- Start the aftermovie process immediately after the trip while files and momentum are fresh.
