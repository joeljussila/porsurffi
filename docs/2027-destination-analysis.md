# PorSurffi 2027 — destination analysis

Prepared 2026-09-21 for the organizer meet on 2026-09-24.

**Constraints used:** depart HEL between **2027-05-03 and 2027-05-10**, trip length **9–12 days**,
**30 people**, all 18+. Level: beginner/intermediate, 0–50 sessions, **average ~7**. Wants waves
with some size and push, not mush, not heavy. Outside Europe, not Sri Lanka, not Portugal.
Ideal: **one supplier for accommodation + surf lessons**, instructors on hand throughout.

---

## The headline

**May is a genuinely good month for surf — but only on the Pacific coast of the Americas and in
Indonesia.** May is the *opening* of the southern-hemisphere swell season, which lights up
Central America, Mexico, Peru and Indonesia simultaneously. It is the *closing* of the season in
Morocco, and the dead middle of the flat season in the Philippines.

That single fact kills two of your six candidates outright.

**The tension you need to resolve on Thursday:** your cheapest flights and your best May waves
point in different directions. Indonesia is the cheap long-haul from Helsinki. Central America
has the better May setup for your exact skill level — long, sand-bottomed point breaks — but
costs more to reach and often routes through the US.

## Eliminated

| Destination | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Siargao** ❌ | **March–June is flat at Cloud 9.** SW monsoon blows onshore, 1–3 ft wind slop, "unsurfable 80% of days". Season is Aug–Nov. This is the clearest no in the set — you would fly 20+ hours to not surf. |
| **Brazil (NE)** ❌ | Pipa/Itacaré peak Dec–Mar on NE trade swell. May is shoulder *and* the tail of the Feb–May rainy period. Florianópolis does peak Apr–Sep but it is far south, cooler, wetsuit territory, and not the tropical trip you're selling. |
| **Morocco** ⚠️ | May is the *end* of the season — 0.5–1.5 m, and June–Sep frequently flat. Fine for pure beginners, too soft for a group averaging 7 sessions who explicitly want more than beginner waves. Also the closest/cheapest option, which you said isn't interesting enough. |
| **Portugal** ❌ | Excluded by you. (For the record, your instinct was right — spring is its weak window, which is why 2026 went to Sri Lanka.) |

## The shortlist

### 1. El Salvador — the closest thing to another Arugam Bay

**May is the start of the season, not the end.** Best surf is May–September on S swells, and the
sweet spot is specifically **May–June**, catching early south swells while it's still relatively
dry.

Why it fits your group unusually well:

- **Las Flores** is a right-hand point that peels ~300 m over a **sand bottom, no rocks or
  reef** — explicitly called out as excellent for beginner *and* intermediate surfers. That is
  structurally the same wave type as Arugam Bay, which you already know works for you.
- **Sunzal** (at El Tunco) is the longest right-hand point in the area — long, mellow, multiple
  sections, "ideal for building confidence".
- Warm water, no wetsuit, and far less commercialised than Costa Rica.

**Watch out for:** the heavier points — Punta Roca, La Bocana, and Las Flores at size — are
intermediate-and-up. And one concrete group problem: **Las Flores Surf Club caps surf passes at
20 per week** to control crowding on the wave. You are 30. That needs a direct conversation
before anything else.

### 2. Nicaragua (Popoyo) — best wind, slightly heavier waves

**April–October is prime**, with SW groundswells giving 4–8 ft faces and water at 27–29 °C.
**May–July is described as the sweet spot for wanting both power and size and clean surf.**

Its unfair advantage is wind: Lake Nicaragua sits just inland and funnels **offshore morning
winds** toward the coast almost daily. Clean faces nearly every morning is rare and valuable.

**Watch out for:** Popoyo is largely **reef**, not sand. For someone on session 3 that is a
meaningfully different proposition from Las Flores. Rainy season also starts in May. Cheap and
uncrowded, though, and there is a real cluster of established camps.

### 3. Lombok (Gerupuk) — the Indonesian answer that isn't Bali

**Dry season May–October**, most consistent swells and offshore easterlies, **2–6 ft**.

**Gerupuk Bay is close to a perfect fit for a mixed-ability group of 30:** five separate breaks
in one bay, all a short boat ride from the village, ranging from *Inside Left* (soft, mellow,
beginner) to *Outside Gerupuk* (intermediate and up). You can put 30 people of wildly different
levels in one bay and everyone gets an appropriate wave. Nothing else on this list does that as
cleanly.

**Watch out for:** winds are technically onshore May–September, but the bay is large and
forgiving enough to stay surfable most mornings — so it's an early-start trip. Also, Lombok camps
run **small-group instruction (often max 2 students per instructor)**; nobody advertises
30-person capacity. That's a direct-enquiry item, not a dealbreaker.

### 4. Costa Rica — the safe one, at a price

May kicks off both the wet season and the swell season; SW swells arrive consistently.
**Nosara / Playa Guiones** is sand-bottomed with multiple peaks suitable for all levels and is
highly consistent; Santa Teresa is punchier. Intermediates are told to target exactly May–October.

Rain is afternoon storms rather than all-day; wind is what actually affects surf quality, not
rain. **The real objection is cost and commercialisation** — it is the most developed and
priciest option here, and you said you don't want the obvious operation.

### 5. Mexico (Pacific) — good, with one sharp caveat

May begins the south-swell season. **La Saladita** is a long, mellow left point running 3–6 ft
from May–September — superb for progression. **Sayulita** is beginner-friendly and May is the end
of high season, so cheaper and quieter.

**Avoid Puerto Escondido.** Zicatela is one of the heaviest beach breaks on earth and is
genuinely dangerous for your level. If Mexico wins, it should be Nayarit or Guerrero, not Oaxaca.

### 6. Bali — works, but it's the obvious one

May is a dry-season shoulder month: clean from first light, swells building, lineups quieter than
peak. April/May is named among the best months to learn. Canggu absorbs every skill level.
Uluwatu is advanced except small days at high tide.

Genuinely good in May, and the cheapest long-haul of the lot. But it's the most obvious,
most crowded, most commercialised answer — and you've said twice you want to avoid that.

## Flights — the honest position

**I cannot give you real May 2027 fares.** What follows is routing structure and typical ranges.
**May 2027 is roughly 8 months out, which means those dates are bookable right now** — so the
team can pull genuine quotes today rather than work from averages. That should happen before
Thursday if anyone has twenty minutes.

| Route | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| **HEL → DPS (Bali)** | No direct; 17 h+ with one stop. Carriers include **Juneyao**, Xiamen, Turkish, Qatar, Emirates, Finnair. **Juneyao is currently the cheapest** — which is exactly the airline Pekka hit the "only 5 seats at a time" limit with. Typical round trip €582–1081, average ≈ €789. |
| **HEL → SJO (Costa Rica)** | Average ≈ €759 connecting. |
| **HEL → SAL / MGA (El Salvador / Nicaragua)** | Thinner and usually pricier. Best non-US routing is via **Madrid (Iberia)** or via **Bogotá/Panama on Avianca** — SAL is an Avianca hub. |

**One thing nobody has flagged yet: US transit requires an ESTA for Finnish passports.** Many
cheap Central America itineraries connect through Miami, Dallas or Atlanta. That is an extra
fee per person plus an approval to chase for 30 people — and 2026 already showed how much
chasing a simple visa approval costs Oona in effort. **Prefer routings via Madrid, Bogotá or
Panama City.** Factor this into any Central America fare comparison; a €40 saving that adds an
ESTA round is not a saving.

Pekka's group-fare enquiry matters more than which destination wins. His own estimate — **700–780 €
for 30 seats** — and your fallback of **9 tickets at a time plus a 20 % risk premium (640 € →
850–900 € with bags)** should both be tested against whichever destination you pick.

## Surf camps — candidates, not recommendations

You asked not to be handed the most obvious operator. I'm listing what exists; **none of these is
vetted and none is confirmed for 30 people.** Ratings need checking by hand, and the "is this
locally run or a foreign-owned brand" question generally cannot be answered from a website.

**El Salvador**
- *Las Flores Surf Club / Las Flores Resort* — run by surfers, in-house surf school, panga fleet
  for boat access to Punta Mango. Explicitly serves groups. **But: 20 surf passes per week.**
- *El Zonte* — quieter village, high-performance right point. One camp there is Lapoint-affiliated
  but independently owned and managed.
- The Las Flores area generally is quieter and more remote than the La Libertad surf towns.

**Nicaragua (Popoyo)** — a real cluster, which is good for negotiating:
- *Nicawaves* — owner is a two-time US Championships finalist, 20 years in Nicaragua, runs 10
  4x4s for spot access.
- *Popoyo Surf Lodge* — the country's first surf camp.
- *Las Plumerias* — oceanfront lodge, coaching plus guided trips to 15+ spots.
- *Popoyo Surf Camp* — all-inclusive, 5/7-night packages, level-split programmes.

**Lombok** — *Nuna Surf* (certified local coaches, video analysis), *LMBK Surf House*,
*DHM Surf Camp* (flexible dates, mentions accommodating special requests), *Feel Lombok*,
*360 Surf School*. All small-group; a 30-person block needs a custom conversation.

**Peru** — *Lobitos Surf Natural* is a **family-run** hostel with meals, accommodation, lessons
and gear included, six breaks nearby. Genuinely the kind of local operation you described
wanting — just check whether it can physically hold 30.

## Recommendation

**Shortlist to two and get quotes: El Salvador and Lombok.**

- **El Salvador** is the wave-quality answer. Las Flores is structurally the same wave that made
  Arugam Bay work for you — a long sand-bottomed right-hand point — and May is the season opening
  rather than closing. The 20-pass cap is the thing to resolve first.
- **Lombok** is the logistics answer. Gerupuk's five-breaks-in-one-bay handles a 30-person group
  with a 50-session spread better than anywhere else here, flights to Indonesia are the cheapest
  long-haul from Helsinki, and it gets you Indonesia without being Bali.

Keep **Nicaragua** as the fallback if El Salvador can't take 30 — same swell window, better wind,
more camp capacity, at the cost of reef instead of sand.

**Do not pick on flight price alone.** Siargao would have been the trap: cheap to reach, and
flat for the entire week you'd be there.
